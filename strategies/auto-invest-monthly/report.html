Setting Up the Auto-Invest System

1
Choose Your Broker — M1 Finance Recommended
M1 Finance is the ideal platform for this strategy. Create a "Pie" with 13 slices at the exact percentages listed. Set a recurring monthly transfer. Auto-invests at target weights including fractional shares — fully hands-off after setup.

Fidelity — Fractional shares, automatic investing feature, zero commissions, excellent DRIP support.
Schwab — Stock Slices for fractional shares; manual per-position setup needed.
M1 Finance handles fractional shares, auto-rebalancing via new contributions, and DRIP all in one place.
2
Set the Recurring Monthly Transfer
Link your bank account. Schedule $500–$1,000 on the 1st of each month (or the 15th as a backup). Make this automatic — the power of DCA comes from investing consistently regardless of market conditions. You'll automatically buy more shares when prices fall.
Remove emotion: invest every month whether markets are up or down. Down months are buying opportunities.
3
Enable DRIP on Dividend Payers
Enable automatic dividend reinvestment (DRIP) for: MSFT, NVDA, AAPL, WMT, LLY, COST, SCHD. Their dividends will auto-purchase more fractional shares, compounding ownership over time. SCHD's 4% yield + 11% dividend growth CAGR alone becomes powerful over a decade.
DRIP can add 20–40% to long-term total return vs. taking dividends as cash.
4
Manually Reinvest QQQI Distributions
QQQI pays monthly distributions (~13–16% annual yield). Do NOT enable DRIP on QQQI. Instead, once a month take those distributions and buy your most underweight position — typically MSFT, NVDA, or SCHD. This redirects high-yield income into your strongest long-term compounders.
At $750/mo into QQQI at 13% yield: expect ~$7–9/month in distributions to redeploy.
5
Quarterly Review — Run /portfolio-review
Every quarter, check allocation drift. If any position has moved ±5% from target, use that month's new contribution to buy the underweight positions — don't sell to rebalance (avoid taxable events). Monitor UNH specifically: watch for DOJ investigation updates and MA cost ratio trends in quarterly earnings.
Use the InvestSkill plugin: /portfolio-review [your holdings] --benchmark QQQ
6
Annual Review + Watchlist Consideration
Every January: reassess UNH (upgrade to 10% if DOJ resolves), check IBIT vs. 10% cap, evaluate if GOOGL/META/V/ABBV have crossed the 7.5+ threshold. Run /research-bundle [ticker] on any new candidates before adding. Keep the total position count under 15 for manageability.
Top watchlist to consider adding: GOOGL, V, META, ABBV — all near the threshold.

Risk Scenarios — Portfolio Stress Tests

Bear Market (–30% equities)

Estimated portfolio drawdown: 22–27%. Gold and SCHD provide ballast. DCA continues buying at lower prices, accelerating recovery. Est. recovery with $750/mo DCA: 18–30 months.

Recession Scenario

WMT + COST + SCHD + IAU = 36% defensive exposure. Consumer staples and gold historically outperform in recessions. NVDA + LLY face multiple compression risk (higher P/E = more to fall).

AI Bubble Burst

MSFT + NVDA + AAPL + AMZN = 38% combined. A 50% tech correction = ~19% portfolio drawdown. Painful but survivable — WMT, COST, SCHD, IAU provide counter-weight.

Inflation Surge

IAU + SLV (13%) + IBIT (7%) = 20% hard-asset exposure. COST and WMT can pass through price increases. QQQI income generation continues. SCHD may underperform.

Tech Sector Rotation

If capital rotates from tech to value/energy: WMT, COST, SCHD, gold outperform. Portfolio is ~36% non-tech, providing natural rotation buffer.

UNH Adverse Outcome

If DOJ forces UNH breakup: 6% position → maximum -6% portfolio impact. Absorbed by conviction holdings. Pre-sized small precisely for this tail risk.

Bitcoin –80% Drawdown

Historical BTC worst-case: –80% from peak. IBIT at 7% × 0.80 loss = 5.6% portfolio hit. Significant but survivable. DCA continues buying into weakness at lower prices.

Geopolitical Crisis

Gold (8%), Silver (5%), and Bitcoin (7%) = 20% non-correlated hard assets. WMT and COST are domestic defensives. This portfolio holds up materially better than a pure equity portfolio in a crisis.
